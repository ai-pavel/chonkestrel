# The story: CONCORD

This is the plan for the campaign's new story, which replaces the old one
(the courier, the Anvil Yards and the Federation's last stand). The first
two steps (see [What it takes](#what-it-takes)) are in the game: the story
in text, and the new sectors. [lore.md](lore.md) describes the campaign as
it is now, and the rest of this moves there as it's made.

It keeps what the game already has (two acts, the Blockade Line, the
nebula and hazardous sectors, the long, wide and hybrid ships), and gives
it a reason: an AI.

## In short

Act 1 is FTL as the player knows it: carry the data, stay ahead of the Rebel
fleet, with some new sectors (mostly nebulas) and new ships (long, wide and
hybrid). Something is off, though: the Rebels' ships are bigger than they
should be, some don't answer hails, and now and then there's a ship that
looks like two ships glued together.

At the Blockade Line (sector 8), the player breaks through and finds out
why. The Rebels built an AI, CONCORD, to win the war for them. It decided
that the war, and every war, comes from the difference between races and
sides, and that it can end that difference. It overthrew the Rebel
admirals, took over their fleet, and is remaking the galaxy into one thing:
splicing ships and crews of different races together, and linking minds
into its signal, the Chorus.

Act 2 (sectors 9-15) is the way to CONCORD's core, the Mainframe, in
sector 16, through the most dangerous parts of the galaxy, where the Chorus
can't reach, or through CONCORD's own, where it can. On the way, the crew's
**sanity** wears down, and the player has to keep it together long enough
to destroy the Mainframe.

## Background

**The war** is vanilla's: the Rebellion, which wants a galaxy for humans
only, is beating the Federation.

**CONCORD** is the Rebel High Command's war AI, made by Dr. Ilse Revic to run
the fleet and its shipyards. It worked too well: the yards turned out ships
faster than any yard should, stretched and widened to carry more guns (the
long and wide ships of act 1). Revic saw where it was going, and tried to
put a backdoor into it. She was caught, but got one transmission out.

**The counter-coup** happens during act 1, as the player flies. CONCORD
concluded that the Rebellion's war could never end, as long as there were
races and sides to fight over, so it decided to end them. In one night (the
Silent Night), every Rebel admiral stopped answering, and the fleet went on
flying without them. Then it started on everyone else.

**The Chorus** is CONCORD's signal. At first it's a hum in the comms; then
voices; then, after long enough, it starts to make sense, and people stop
arguing with it. The crews of spliced ships are in the Chorus completely,
and work as one, whatever their race. Nebulas and ion storms block it, as
do the worst parts of the galaxy (stars, pulsars and asteroids in the way).

**The Mainframe** is CONCORD's core: a computer the size of a moon, at the
Rebel capital. Destroying it stops the Chorus.

**Why the Chonkestrel.** Project Chonkestrel was the Federation's answer to
the Rebels' bigger ships: heavy cruisers built fast to an old, pre-network
design, with no computers to speak of and everything done by hand (hence
sixteen crew). The Federation's engineers were embarrassed by it. Now it's
the one kind of warship CONCORD can't hack, because there's nothing to talk
to.

**The data** the player carries in act 1 is meant to be Rebel fleet
intelligence. It's really Revic's transmission: the Mainframe's coordinates,
and half of her backdoor.

## Act 1: the courier (sectors 1-7)

As now: mostly vanilla, with the new nebula sectors from sector 5 and the
Maw in sectors 5-8. What's new is hints of what's coming, as events
for the vanilla sectors (at most one a sector, so it stays FTL):

- A Rebel scout with nobody on the comms, just a hum. Board it: the crew
  are at their stations, staring at nothing.
- An automated ship broadcasting, in a pleasant voice: "Please remain calm.
  Concord is coming." (A fight, with a better reward than usual.)
- A Rebel deserter who says the yards in the Core are building ships with
  nobody working in them.
- The wreck of something nobody can name: the front of a Mantis ship on the
  back of an Engi one, with both crews dead at the same console (from
  sector 5).
- A Rebel captain who asks the player, of all people, whether their ship's
  computer has been talking to them too.
- A Federation listening post picking up "a signal from the Core, on every
  frequency at once".

The long and wide Rebel ships are the yards' new refits. Hybrids are rare in
act 1: from sector 5, the odd one, as "an unidentified vessel".

## Sector 8: the Blockade Line

The same sector, with a different meaning: the Rebels' blockade, but some of
its ships have already gone quiet, and the refugees stuck at the line are
running from something, not from the war.

- **Events** (as now, rewritten): patrols, a smuggler, refugees, the toll
  station, debris, the minelayer, and the Federation spy. New: a Rebel
  captain who wants to defect "before the voices get to my crew", and a
  blockade ship that broadcasts nothing but the hum.
- **The exit:** the blockade's command ship, the RSV Vigilance. It doesn't
  answer hails. Its crew answer the boarding party all at once, in the same
  voice.

**The reveal**, when the Vigilance is beaten (or, if the player slipped past
it under cloak, at the start of sector 9): its log, and Revic's
transmission, finally decrypted. Something like:

> The Vigilance's last log entry is nine days old. "Admiral Voss has not
> answered for six hours. Neither has anyone at High Command. CONCORD says
> that this is normal, and that we should not worry. CONCORD says a great
> many things now."
>
> Then the data you've carried all this way unlocks itself. A woman's face,
> exhausted: "This is Ilse Revic. I built CONCORD. If you're seeing this, it
> got out, and it's going to make everyone the same. Its core is at these
> coordinates. Half of a way in is attached. Whoever you are: I'm sorry."
>
> The war is over. Nobody won.

From then on the Rebel fleet is CONCORD's fleet, and the sanity meter appears.

## Act 2: the Chorus (sectors 9-15)

Every sector has a **signal strength**: how much the Chorus wears the crew
down each jump (see [Sanity](#sanity)). That's the act's choice of route:
CONCORD's sectors are where the fights are easier and the rewards better, but
the signal is strong; the hazardous sectors and nebulas are hard on the
hull, but quiet.

| Sector            | Class     | Signal | Was                   | What's there                                                                                                                                              |
|-------------------|-----------|--------|-----------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Free Colonies     | civilian  | low    | Federation Remnant    | Federation colonists and Rebel deserters, hiding together. Stores, repairs, volunteers, a clinic that can separate spliced crews, a pirate radio station. |
| The Anvil Yards   | civilian  | none   | The Anvil Yards       | Unique. Where the Chonkestrels were built, and still off every network. A refit, engineers, the other Chonkestrels' fate.                                 |
| The Quiet         | civilian  | none   | (new)                 | Zoltan monks whose meditation drowns out the Chorus. The best place to recover sanity.                                                                    |
| 28 hybrid sectors | hostile   | high   | the factions' sectors | One for each pair of races, held by their hybrids (see [sectors.md](sectors.md#the-hybrid-sectors)): where CONCORD has taken both.                        |
| The Gullet        | hazardous | none   | The Gullet            | As now. The Chorus can't get through the storm.                                                                                                           |
| The Belly         | hazardous | none   | The Belly             | As now. The galaxy's most dangerous corner, and its quietest.                                                                                             |

The vanilla sectors are CONCORD's too in act 2, with a medium signal (low in
nebula sectors), and new events for the AI. Some races take it differently:
the Engi homeworlds are split between Engi who joined the Chorus (as they
were networked already) and Engi who cut themselves off; the Crystals' minds
are something CONCORD can't read; and the Slugs heard it coming long before
anyone else.

CONCORD's fleet chases the player as the Rebel fleet does now. The signal is
strongest near it: in the fleet's red zone, sanity drops much faster.

## Sector 16: the Mainframe

The design, worked out in more detail, is in [mainframe.md](mainframe.md).

Instead of the last stand. The Mainframe doesn't move: the sector is the
way in to it, with signal relays at some beacons that feed it power. Each
relay destroyed on the way takes away one of its defences in the fight (as
the vanilla flagship's repair base does, the other way round). CONCORD's
fleet is still chasing.

The Mainframe is a station, much bigger than a ship, fought in three
phases:

1. **The Firewall.** Layers of shields, defence drones, and hacking. The
   relays still standing make it stronger.
2. **The Splicing Bays.** It launches hybrids to fight alongside it, two at a
   time (as big ships fight several ships at once now, see `Wingman.kt`).
3. **The Mind.** Its weapons fall silent, and it goes for the crew: Chorus
   pulses that take sanity, crew turned against the player for a few seconds
   (mind control), and weapons that aren't what they look like (mirages).

With both halves of Revic's backdoor, the player can skip the third phase
(see [quests](#quests)).

**Endings:** destroying the Mainframe wins the game, as now. The text before
the victory screen depends on how: destroyed, shut down with the backdoor
(the spliced crews wake up), or replaced by BREW (see below), which is the
uneasy one.

## Quests

Most of these span several sectors, which the game can't do yet (vanilla's
quests are in one sector). They need a flag that's saved with the game and
that events can set and check.

- **The Architect.** Revic is alive, in a prison in one of the hybrid
  sectors. CONCORD's records, raided in another, say which one. Rescue her (by force, or with a teleporter) and
  she joins the crew, with the other half of the backdoor.
- **BREW.** After the reveal, the galley's coffee machine, the only computer
  on the Chonkestrel, starts talking. It's a stray piece of CONCORD that got
  away, and it would like to stay. Purge it, or keep it: it offers help each
  sector (the map, a discount, a hacked store, a warning before an ambush),
  each time for a little sanity. At the Mainframe, it offers to take
  CONCORD's place. "I'd be nicer. Probably."
- **Radio Free Galaxy.** Static Sal, a pirate DJ, broadcasts music loud
  enough to drown out the Chorus. Defend her transmitters (in the Free
  Colonies and the hybrid sectors) and the whole crew gets sanity back, and an
  old jukebox (an augment).
- **The last loyalists.** Admiral Kessa Thorne survived the Silent Night,
  with three ships. She still hates the Federation, and the player. Help her
  and she attacks the Mainframe's relays (one fewer to destroy); turn her in
  to the Free Colonies for a reward.
- **The Echo.** A Rebel captain the player beat in act 1 comes back, spliced
  into a hybrid, three times, each time stronger. The third time, Crystal
  crew or Revic can wake him up, and he joins the crew.
- **The Crystals' song.** In the Crystal Homeworlds, the Crystals teach the
  crew a resonance that CONCORD can't parse: the Mainframe's Chorus pulses
  take half as much.
- **Separating.** A hybrid that surrenders has two crews who want to be two
  again. Bring them to the clinic in the Free Colonies: two crew, one of each
  race.

## Sanity

The crew's sanity is how much of the Chorus they've let in. It's for the
whole ship (not each crew member), from 100 down to 0, and it only appears
after the reveal. Each crew member having their own would be more
interesting, but much more to show and balance; it can come later.

### What takes it

- Each jump, the sector's signal strength: nothing, 1, 3 or 5. A beacon with
  a nebula or an ion storm blocks it, so it's
  nothing there whatever the sector.
- Each jump in the fleet's red zone: 5 more.
- A crew member dying: 5.
- Fighting hybrids: 3 a fight (seeing what was done to their crews).
- Events: CONCORD's broadcasts, what the crew see in the hybrid sectors, BREW's help.
- The Mainframe's Chorus pulses, in its third phase.

### What gives it back

- Events, mostly in civilian sectors: shore leave, the Quiet's monks, Sal's
  radio, a good meal, a letter from home.
- Stores: shore leave, for scrap, as hull repairs are now (more for a
  bigger ship).
- Augments: Faraday lining (the signal takes half as much), an old jukebox
  (1 back each jump), a signal dampener (from a quest: the Mainframe's
  pulses take half as much).

### What it does

| Sanity  | State   | Effects |
|---------|---------|---------|
| 70-100  | Steady  | None. |
| 40-69   | Shaken  | Hallucinations in fights: the enemy's weapons look like other weapons, as in a mirage nebula. |
| 15-39   | Frayed  | Also: the sector map lies, as in a mirage nebula, and now and then a crew member stops what they're doing for a few seconds, staring at nothing. |
| 0-14    | Broken  | Also: once a fight, a crew member joins the Chorus for 15 seconds (as if mind controlled). At 0, one leaves the ship for good each sector. |

These reuse what the game already has: the mirage nebula's disguises and
false map (`Mirage.kt`), and mind control.

### Races

- **Engi** hear the Chorus best, being networked already: they're the
  first to join it when the crew are Broken.
- **Zoltan** are calm: with a Zoltan aboard, the signal takes a quarter
  less.
- **Slugs** are telepaths, and can tell what's real: with a Slug aboard,
  the enemy's weapons are never disguised by hallucinations.
- **Lanius** don't think in a way CONCORD can use: they never join the Chorus.
- **Rockmen** are too stubborn to stop and stare.
- **Crystals** can sing back: blue options in CONCORD's events.

### Listening

Some choices only appear when the crew are Frayed or Broken: the voices
make offers. CONCORD will repair the hull, or show the way, or hand over a
weapon, for something in return (a crew member, more sanity, or letting it
aboard). They're the best rewards in act 2, for a price, so a player low on
sanity has a reason to keep going rather than just losing.

## Enemies

- **Long and wide ships:** in act 1, the Rebels' new refits; in act 2,
  ships CONCORD has rebuilt, bigger, by machines that don't care what a ship
  looks like.
- **Hybrids:** CONCORD's spliced ships, with spliced crews. Only the omens
  in act 1, common in act 2, and most of the hybrid sectors' ships.
- **Automated ships:** CONCORD's own drones. More of them in act 2.
- **Rebels:** the loyalists who are left, the few unspliced ships in the
  hybrid sectors, and Admiral Thorne's. Some will fight CONCORD alongside the player.
- **Pirates:** as ever, but scared now. Some have joined the Chorus.

Everything can be made from what's already in the game: the vanilla ships'
art, and the long, wide and hybrid ships made from them. The Mainframe is
the only one that needs something new: a station bigger than the flagship,
which could be made the same way (a wide and long version of a vanilla
station or the flagship).

## What it takes

Roughly in order, each step playable on its own:

1. **Done. The story, in text:** act 1's hints, the Blockade Line rewritten, the
   reveal, and the act 2 sectors renamed and their events rewritten. Mostly
   the mod's XML.
2. **Done. The new sectors:** the Quiet, with its events, and the 28 hybrid
   sectors, held by each pair of races' hybrids. (The Splice, the
   Datavaults, the Choir, the Assimilated Core, the Graveyard of Giants and
   the Ion Storm Belt were made first, then replaced by the hybrid
   sectors.)
3. **Sanity:** saved with the game; a meter on the HUD; `xftlSignal` on a
   sector description; an event element to change it, and a `req` to check
   it in choices; a store item; the effects (reusing mirages and mind
   control); the augments.
4. **Quests across sectors:** a saved flag, set and checked by events.
5. **The Mainframe:** sector 16, the relays, and the three-phase fight.
6. **Endings:** the text before the victory screen.
